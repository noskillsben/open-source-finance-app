# Using the app — point-form guide

One bullet per thing you can do, in the order you'd meet it. Kept current as the app is built: every closed issue that changes what a user can do adds or edits a bullet here. Long-form pages grow out of this file when there is enough to say.

## Getting started

- Run `docker compose up --build` and open http://localhost:5173.
- Pick a date with the "Show as of" control in the header to see every balance as it stood that day — it also becomes the default date on every form (accounts and transactions) — on Transactions, changing it updates the Record form's date at once, replacing a date you typed, though a transaction you are editing keeps its own date — and the category and payee lists only show what existed that day, until you change it again. It stays on that date when you reload, until you change it or open a new tab. When the date is not today, the control is outlined and a "not today" note appears beside it, with a "Today" button that puts it back to today in one tap.
- Move around the app from the menu, grouped by what you're doing: **Record** (Transactions), **Assign** (Categories, Accounts, Payees, Domains), **Plan** (Income, Due dates, Split expenses), and **Settings** (Data check). **Review** shows as a header with nothing under it yet — its pages arrive with later issues. On a desktop the menu is a sidebar with Settings at its foot, and "Show as of" and "+ Add transaction" sit in the top bar. On a phone the menu becomes a bar at the bottom — Categories, Transactions, +, Due dates, More, each an icon over its label and the + a round button (Quick add) — and "More" opens the grouped menu; "Due dates" opens that page. The bottom bar hides while a form is open and the last row of every page scrolls clear of it. The app is one dark theme, set in IBM Plex Sans, and follows your browser's and phone's text-size setting.
- Narrow any list by typing or choosing a filter: Accounts by name, type and side (on-budget or tracking), Categories by name, need level, goal kind (or no goal) and an "Overspent" box that shows only categories whose available balance is negative, and Income by name and how often, and Transactions by a search of the memo and payee plus account, category, payee, domain and need level. "Clear filters" appears while anything is narrowing the list and puts everything back. Sort Categories, Accounts, Income and Due dates by tapping a column header: tap again to reverse, and the arrow on the header always shows the order you are looking at (Categories sort inside each group, with a group ranked by its total). "Clear filters" puts the default order back too. Your filters and sort are remembered while the tab is open and never saved to your data. Wherever you choose from a long list (the category or account on a transaction line, an envelope, the category for a balance-check difference) you type to search it instead of scrolling a drop-down, and the suggestions are alphabetical. Short fixed lists stay drop-downs, in alphabetical order, except need levels (need, should, nice to have, want) and account types, which are grouped on-budget first and then tracking.
- Add and edit forms open in a panel beside the list (full screen on a phone): the list stays visible with the row highlighted, and the panel closes on save, Cancel, Escape or ×. To enter several in a row, use Quick add's Add another. Money boxes show the decimal keypad, and where a direction matters a "− Spent / + Received" toggle sets the sign.
- The payee "Me" already exists the first time you open the app — no need to add it before recording a transfer between your own accounts.
- The category "Debt payments" also exists the first time you open the app (loan accounts pre-select it); there are no other starter categories or domains, so you add your own. Rename or archive it and it won't come back.

## Accounts

- Add an account: give it a name, a type (Cash, Chequing, Savings, Credit card, Gift card / store credit, Money owed to me, Line of credit, Mortgage, Payment plan, Loan, Investment, or Asset), whether it's on-budget or tracking, an on-budget floor (for overdraft or budgeting with credit — shown only while the account is on-budget), a credit limit, and an opening balance dated whenever you like (leave it blank and it saves as 0.00). Type the balance without a sign and tap **− Owed** or **+ Held**: a card with $500 on it is 500 with Owed chosen, and the floor works the same way. The account type never picks the side for you. Transactions dated before that balance are absorbed into it, so you can enter backfilled history in any order and today's balance never changes. It shows up right away in the account list with its type, side, and balance, highlighted, with "Recorded." above the list (the same goes for a new category, domain, named pay or split). You can also lock an account to a payee (gift cards, store credit); a gift card uses the Gift card / store credit type, which starts on-budget with a floor and credit limit of 0 and puts the lock field first; if the store isn't a payee yet, type its name in the lock box and tap **+ add payee** (or press Enter) to create it on the spot. Spending from it pre-fills that payee, and naming a different payee on money going out of it shows a note — a warning, never a refusal. A physical card and an app balance at the same store, locked to the same payee, show as one line with a balance each. The account list is grouped by budget side (On-budget, then Tracking) or, with **Group by**, by type in the type list's order; the column you grouped by is dropped, since the group header says it. Archived accounts (with "Show archived") sit in one Archived group at the end either way. Each group header shows the group's total balance under Balance; click it to collapse or open the group, or use **Expand all** / **Collapse all**. Searching or filtering opens every group holding a match. Sorting reorders inside each group, and accounts locked to the same payee stay together within their group. The grouping and which groups are collapsed are remembered on this device, and "Clear filters" puts the grouping back to Budget side. Cashback that unlocks later is two ordinary pieces: a tracking account you record each month's cashback on, then a transfer dated January 1 into an on-budget account locked to that payee. Until that date the cashback counts in net worth only; on January 1 it arrives in ready to assign, and nothing special fires.
- Edit an account: click it in the account list to change its name, type, on-budget/tracking side, or floor, and save. Only transactions recorded after the change use the new setting — nothing already recorded is touched. Renaming to a name already in use is refused. If the account has a stated credit limit, the floor can't be set below it — you can't budget with credit the lender hasn't extended.
- Credit limit: on the account form, state the limit your lender set. Cash, Chequing and Savings start at 0 (no credit); the credit and loan types start blank, meaning unknown — the box says "unknown" rather than showing 0.00, because blank and 0 are different answers, and blank says nothing. A balance past the limit warns rather than blocks: on the transaction that caused it, and as a standing note under the account's name in the account list. A floor set below a stated limit is refused.
- Loan terms: on the account form, record what the lender set — annual rate (a percent, kept to four decimal places), how it compounds (daily, monthly or semi-annual), the day the statement closes (1 to 31) and the grace days after it, when the term and the amortization end, the prepayment kind (open, closed with privileges, or penalty), a promo's end date and its deferred rate, and the minimum-payment rule in your own words. The section starts folded for every account type, under a one-line summary of what is stated so far ("Nothing entered" when empty); click "Loan terms" to unfold it. Leave a box blank when you don't know it — blank means unknown, and a typed 0 means 0 (a 0% plan, no grace days) — nothing is filled in for you. A rate below 0, a close day outside 1 to 31 or negative grace days is refused. What you've entered shows as a soft line under the account's name in the account list, e.g. "5.99% · compounds daily · statement closes the 15th, 21 days grace"; editing other things on the account keeps the terms you've stated.
- Carrying a balance: an account with a statement-close day and grace days says so on its row when part of a statement is still unpaid after its due date — "Carrying $230.00 from the Sep 15 statement (due Oct 10)." It counts every payment or refund since the statement closed, so paying the rest late clears it, and a card you pay in full every month never shows it. It stays quiet until the due date has passed, and when either of the two terms is blank. It follows the "Show as of" date, and it's only a note: nothing is blocked.
- Money leaving the budget for a loan: on the account form, pick the category money leaves your budget through when you pay into that account (the box is only there while the account is tracking, the one case that reads it). A new Line of credit, Mortgage, Payment plan or Loan starts with "Debt payments" there; other types start blank, and accounts you already have are not changed. On the transaction form, a transfer from an on-budget account into a tracking account with a category set fills in two lines: that category for what landed on the loan, and a second line for the rest of what left your budget, with no category (chequing -450, Car loan +380 pre-fills Debt payments -380 and a -70 line you categorise yourself, e.g. Interest). When the payment lands whole (chequing -450, Line of credit +450) there is only the one line, Debt payments -450. Money coming into your budget (a line of credit draw, a sale) fills in nothing and arrives in ready to assign. It is only a pre-fill: you can change or clear it, and nothing already recorded is touched.
- Lend someone money: make an account of type Money owed to me for the borrower, lock it to their payee, fill in the terms you set (rate, term end), and check **Linked to**: the form pre-fills the Owed to me category, and you change it to the category the money came from if you want that category to keep holding the loan. Lending is an ordinary transfer from your chequing account into it (for $2,000, chequing -2,000, Friend +2,000). The form asks which category it funds, pre-filled with the one the account is linked to when there is only one: pick "already earmarked" (empty the step) or the category it came from, and Emergency fund keeps holding the $2,000 while it is out, and spending it tells you the money is with Friend (a warning, never a refusal). A repayment is a transfer back: Friend -160, chequing +165, with Interest income +5 as a category line. The form asks which category the principal returns to (the one it came from, ready to assign, or none). Interest is recorded when you are paid it, never worked out for you. Even if the borrower is also in a split, a loan on terms gets its own account.
- Front money for a friend: make an account of type Money owed to me named after them (the form pre-fills **Linked to** with Owed to me; you can change or clear it, and it only shows while the account is on-budget), then record what you paid as a transfer from the account you paid with into theirs (for $40, chequing -40, Friend +40). The form then asks which envelope it funds, pre-filled to move the $40 from ready to assign into Owed to me, so ready to assign shows only money you hold. Paying you back is a transfer out of it, and the step moves the $40 back. Links on an existing account are changed from the category's "Money lives in", not from the account's edit form.
- Archive an account: click "Archive" on its row, confirm the date (it starts as the "Show as of" date), and save. Any warning — for example a balance that isn't zero on that date — is shown, but the account is archived regardless. It can't be archived on or before the date of a transaction that uses it, and it's refused while it holds someone's balance in a live split — take them out on the Split expenses page first. Tick "Show archived" above the list to see archived accounts and "Unarchive" one; that's refused if another account now has the same name.

## Categories and goals

- Add a category: give it a name and, if you like, the category it sits under, a domain, a need level (need, should, nice to have or want) and a pool — the category it draws on when it overspends — and *absorb overspending* (off by default; see "Overspend from a pool"). Every category can be posted to, whether or not it has sub-categories underneath. Press "Add a category" to open the form; click a row to change any of these, including its name. A category can't be its own pool, directly or through a chain of pools, and can't sit under itself or one of its own sub-categories. A name already in use is refused, ignoring capital letters.
- Domains are a label for grouping categories in reports (Food might hold Groceries and Dining out). Add, rename or archive them on the Domains page (Assign → Domains); archived domains free up their name, and "Show archived" brings them back to unarchive.
- See your categories in the Categories list, with sub-categories indented under their parent, and each one's domain and need level beside it. Click a row to open it in the side panel, where **Edit** is the form and **Archive** sits at the foot; Archive archives it as of the "Show as of" date. If the category holds money, or owes it, that balance moves to Ready to assign and a note says how much ("moved $42.10 to Ready to assign"); a category archived with children moves each one's balance the same way. Tick "Show archived" to see archived categories and "Unarchive" one; that's refused if another category now has the same name.
- Collapse a category group: a category with sub-categories shows a chevron; click it or the category's name to fold its sub-categories away or open them again. The parent's Available is always the group total (its own money plus every sub-category's), and when the parent holds money of its own a first line such as "Food itself" shows that part. Groups start open; which ones you folded is remembered on this device. "Expand all" and "Collapse all" sit beside the search box and "Show archived", and searching by name opens every group that holds a match.
- Assign money to a category: the Categories page opens with "Ready to assign $X" — all the money in your on-budget accounts that no category holds yet, as of the "Show as of" date. Press **Move money** on a category's row (on a phone, tap the row) and the side panel opens at an amount: type it and press **Add from ready to assign** to move it from ready to assign into the category, dated the "Show as of" date, and the category's Available column and the headline both update, or **Withdraw to ready to assign** to move it back out. A short line above the amount says which button does what. To move money from one category into another, pick the other category under "Move to…" (searchable; captioned as another-category only), and press Move; ready to assign doesn't change. Moves never block: a category can go negative. Spending on a category that has money assigned leaves ready to assign alone. If a category has spent more than it holds, its Available shows negative and the headline adds "(includes -$X in overspent categories)": that money is still counted in ready to assign until you cover the overspend. An archived category can't be moved into or out of.
- Overspend from a pool: when a category has a pool and you record spending that is more than the category holds, the shortfall is covered from the pool automatically, then from the pool's own pool, and so on up the chain, and only what none of them can cover leaves the category negative — unless a pool along the way is set to *absorb overspending*. A pool never goes negative because of this unless it absorbs: then what the whole chain could not cover lands on the first absorbing pool the chain passed, which goes negative instead, so you refill once, at the pool. The category still records the full spend, so spent and reports stay accurate, and it shows zero available. Idle money further up the chain is always used first, turning the switch on or off changes only spending recorded afterwards, and it does nothing on the category being spent on itself. The transaction shows a note such as "Snacks: covered $40.00 from Food, then $20.00 from Household." (or, when a pool absorbed the rest, "…; Food absorbed $25.00 (now −$25.00)."), ready to assign doesn't change, and editing or deleting the transaction redoes or removes that cover. Changing a category's pool later doesn't touch cover already given. A category with a pool shows "+$X available if overspent" under its Available amount: what the pool chain could cover right now, with ", then Food absorbs the rest" when a pool in the chain absorbs.
- Set a goal on a category: click the category's row and use the Goal section. Pick a recurring bill (an amount, how often — monthly, quarterly, every 6 months, yearly, or every N weeks — and its first due date — required; later due dates follow from it by the cadence, and changing the date or the cadence restarts them from the new first date), a target (an amount to reach, optionally a date, and if there is a date, optionally how often you want to set money aside — a target is paced by its named pay or by its own how-often, not both: picking a named pay hides how often), or a commitment (a standing rule with no end: add a fixed amount, add a percentage of net, or refill to a level). A fixed-amount commitment asks how often: *Each payday* (the full amount on every pay, nothing else to fill in), *Monthly*, *Quarterly*, *Every 6 months* or *Yearly*; for the last four you also pick the first month it is due (required), and its due dates are the end of that month, stepped forward by the cadence. There is no every-N-weeks for a commitment (bills keep it). Refill and percent-of-net commitments are always per payday and don't ask. A fixed-amount commitment's goal row reads "$200.00 monthly · next due Oct 31" (or "$50.00 each payday"), and its progress is what has been assigned to the category since the period began, up to the amount: "$140.00 of $200.00 this month" (this quarter, this 6-month period, this year). Spending from the category doesn't lower it, and it needs no named pay; "each payday" has no period, so its row shows just that. Refill and percent-of-net rows show no cadence. Give it its own name, such as "My part of the car insurance". Any goal can name the named pay that pays it, searchable in the same form; a commitment that adds a fixed amount can instead add a percentage of that pay's net ("5% of net"), never both — the percentage needs a named pay picked first. A recurring bill with a named pay picked also offers a checkbox, "Set aside whole on the last payday before it's due": ticked, the bill is not spread over several paydays; the pay screen fills in nothing until the last payday on or before its due date, and that pay fills in everything still missing. Unticked (the default) it is split across the paydays as before; clearing the named pay clears the box. A category holds one goal — for a second bill, add a sub-category with its own goal; choose "No goal" and save to remove it. A goal's named pay is optional: leave it blank for a bill funded through a pool, and the goal then shows as an upcoming deadline and never pre-fills on the pay screen. The goal shows as a row under the category: its name, "$132.82 of $1,048.00" (the category's balance against the goal as of the "Show as of" date), the due date, and what to set aside each period (a target's is rounded up to the cent; a target with a date and a named pay reads "$93.30 per Salary payday", the same amount the pay screen fills in, and a target with a named pay and no date shows none). The goal row has no amount box; its only control is Record on a recurring bill, and money moves through the category row's Move money.
- See whether each recurring bill is paid, due or overdue: its goal row on Categories also says its amount and how often it repeats ("$87.00 yearly", "$120.00 every 2 weeks"), and reads "overdue since Oct 1" (the picker date is past its earliest unpaid due date), "due Oct 1 · not paid" (that date is before the next payday of the named pay that pays it, or, for a bill with no named pay, before the next payday of any of your named pays, or on its due date if you have none) or "next due Nov 1". A bill that is due or overdue also says how much its envelope is still short of the bill's amount ("due Oct 1 · not paid · $300.00 short"); the note leaves once the envelope holds the whole amount, and "next due" never carries it. A bill due exactly on that payday reads "next due" until the payday arrives; on payday itself, the next payday is the one still to come, not today. An unpaid bill stays due until a payment is marked as paying it; it never rolls on to next month by itself. Once a payment has been marked, the row also shows what was paid against the last paid due date next to what was expected ("Oct 1 paid · $112.40 of $120.00"), so a half payment stays visible. On Income, a bill is listed at that same due date, so an overdue bill shows there too, sorted with the ones due before the next payday first.
- A goal whose category has gone below zero reads "$525.00 short" in red on its goal row, rather than "−$525.00 of $1,050.00".
- Record a bill payment in one tap: every recurring bill's goal row on Categories has a "Record Oct 1" link naming its earliest unpaid due date. It opens the Transactions form already filled in and marked as paying that bill and due date: the bill's category and expected amount (as money going out), the "Show as of" date (the day the money moves, so paying early or late is fine), and the payee and account of the bill's last marked payment (both empty the first time). Change anything, then save it yourself; the app never records a payment on its own. Once saved, the link reads the bill's next unpaid due date.
- Add a transaction from your phone in a few taps on **Quick add** (the round + in the bottom bar, or Quick add under Record in the menu). Tap one of your six most recent payees, or search for one; type the amount (− Spent is already chosen, switch to + Received for money in); the category and account fill in from your last payment to that payee and the date is Show as of, with a line saying where they came from, and each is one tap (Change) away. Add a one-line Memo if you want one (optional). Press Save. A note says what was recorded, with **Add another** and **Done** (back to the page you came from). If the category is now overspent the note warns you and says by how much. If the category has a recurring bill, it asks "This pays Hydro, due Oct 1? Yes / No" before saving, and only Yes links it. If your last payment to that payee was a shared bill, Quick add shows one line and opens the full Transactions form instead. **More details** opens the Transactions form with everything you typed already in it, memo included; **Add another** starts with an empty memo.
- See everything with a due date in one place: **Due dates** under Plan lists every recurring bill and every fixed set-aside that has a due month (a fixed-amount Commitment with a cadence and first due month), soonest next due first, so anything overdue leads. Each row shows its name and category, its amount and how often ("$120.00 monthly"), its status (a bill's "overdue since Oct 1", "due Oct 1 · not paid · $300.00 short" (the short note is how much the envelope still lacks of the bill) or "next due Nov 1"; a set-aside's "next due Mar 31") and what is saved, the same numbers as its goal row on Categories. Bills have a **Record** link that opens the Transactions form filled in, as on Categories; set-asides have none, because that money is set aside, not paid. Filter by bills or set-asides, by category (searchable) and by how often. **Add** asks whether it's a bill or a set-aside, then opens the goal form with a category picker that can also add the category; click any row to open the same form. Targets and Commitments without a due month (each payday, refill to a level, percent of net) are not listed here; they stay on Categories.
- Link a category to the accounts its money lives in: click the category's row and tick the on-budget accounts under "Money lives in" — Home Downpayment can sit in two FHSAs, and a TFSA can hold both E-bike and Vacation. A tracking account can't be linked, and archiving either end removes the link. A linked category shows "money is in TFSA" under its name, and every account in a group of linked accounts and categories shows the same note when their totals drift apart ("E-bike and Vacation hold $500.00 more than TFSA does", "TFSA and FHSA hold $120.00 not claimed by E-bike and Vacation", or "Chequing and Sam loan hold $3,150.00 not claimed by Emergency fund" on both accounts) — a reminder only, nothing is blocked or corrected. When you record anything that puts money into a linked account — a transfer, a shared bill, a settle-up — the form asks which envelope it funds (when the account is linked to just one category, the step comes pre-filled and you can change it; only the part of the balance above zero counts, so owing someone moves nothing, and an account below zero counts as holding nothing in the note above): pick a linked category, an amount and where the money comes from (ready to assign, or another category), add more envelopes to split it ($50 / $450), or leave it empty if the money was already earmarked. A transfer out of a linked account asks the mirror question. The amounts can add up to less than the transfer but not more, and editing or deleting the transfer redoes or removes them. Spending from a linked category out of some other account, or withdrawing money from one, tells you the money is in the linked account — a warning, never a refusal. On a goal due within five years, linking an Investment account shows a note that the money may not be there when the date comes.

## Payees

- See your payees in the Payees list. Click "Archive" on a row to archive it as of the "Show as of" date (the payee "Me" can't be archived, and neither can anyone who's in a live split — take them out on the Split expenses page first). Tick "Show archived" to see archived payees and "Unarchive" one; that's refused if another payee now has the same name.

## Split expenses

- Set up a split on the Split expenses page under Plan: give it a name (and a description if you like), then add the other people in it and a percent for each. You are never in the list: your share is whatever they leave, and it updates under the form as you type ("Your share: 50%"). A roommate at 50% leaves you 50%. If the people add up to more than 100% the form says so and the save is refused; exactly 100% is fine, which leaves you 0%. Percents are kept to four decimal places.
- Pick each person from the searchable list, or type a new name to add them as a payee. The first time someone joins a split, an on-budget account of type Money owed to me, named after them, is created to hold their balance, linked to the Owed to me category; if they are already in another split, that same account is used, so one person has one balance across all your splits. Someone you take out and add back later keeps their old account, as long as it hasn't been archived. You can instead pick an account you already have for them. A name that matches an account you already have is refused with a note to pick that account.
- Click "Edit" on a split to change its name, its people or their percentages, or take someone out. Changing a split never touches anything already recorded. If someone you take out (or whose split you archive) still owes you, or you owe them, the page says so — "Sam still owes you $60.00; their balance stays on Accounts." — and the change is saved regardless; nothing is said if they're still in another live split. "Archive" removes a split (its name frees up for reuse), but a split can't be archived on or before the date of a bill that used it; tick "Show archived" to see archived splits and "Unarchive" one, which is refused if another split now has the same name. A split is only a rule; recording a shared bill is done on the Transactions form, below.
- Record a shared bill on the Transactions form: pick the split, type the bill's Amount (the whole bill: Spent for a bill you paid, Received for a refund on one), and the lines fill in from the percentages. Say the bill is $100 at 50/50 and you paid it: your account goes down $100, your roommate's account goes up $50 (what they owe you), and the category gets $50. The form then asks which envelope their $50 funds, pre-filled to move $50 from ready to assign into Owed to me, so ready to assign falls by what they owe you; clear it if you would rather not set it aside. To cover several categories, add more category lines and type the receipt amount for each — your share is spread across them, and Amount becomes their sum (a $150 run as Groceries 100 and Household 50 puts $50 and $25 on the categories at 50/50). Every amount can still be changed before you save. The split's name shows on the Transactions row ("Split: Household").
- Paid by starts as Me. Pick someone else from the split when they paid: nothing comes out of your accounts, your share goes on the category, and the same amount comes off their account (you owe them). The category has to hold your share that day, as if you had paid. The Amount (the whole bill) is kept with a bill someone else paid, so changing it later rescales your share by what that bill's share was, even if the split has changed since; a bill saved before this keeps its amounts for editing by hand with the Amount read-only. Paid by is only there once a split is chosen. When someone else paid and you still owe them, nothing moves into Owed to me — owing them already holds that money back.
- Only your share counts as spending. Their part of the bill sits on their account with no category, so every spending report shows your $50 of a $100 meal, never the $100. Editing a saved shared bill and changing the total rescales it by the shares already on that bill, so a one-off 60/40 stays 60/40, and changing a split later never touches a bill already recorded.
- Settle up on the Split expenses page: under each person you see what they owe you (or what you owe them) and the transactions that built that balance, since it last stood at zero. A person who has never owed anything reads "Nothing owed"; "Settled up" appears once something has been settled. A person in two splits shows the same balance under each. Press "Settle up" and the Transactions form opens as a plain transfer between their account and yours for the full balance, with no category, and the envelope step pre-filled to move that amount from Owed to me back into ready to assign: change the amount if they are paying only part, pick your own account, and save. A part payment leaves the list running until the balance clears; nothing marks a transfer as a settle-up. Nothing is shown to settle when the balance is zero.
- Write off by hand: a debt that will never be repaid is an ordinary transaction on Transactions, from their account to a category you choose (say Gifts or Bad debt). It brings their balance back to zero and the envelope step moves the amount out of Owed to me; the app never writes or suggests it.
- Plan a recurring shared bill at your share, not the full amount: "paid against" adds up the bill's own category lines, which are your share only, so a full-rent bill amount would look half-paid forever.

## Recording money

- Record a transaction: open the form with **+ Add transaction**, a bill's Record link, Settle up, or by tapping a row to edit it. The form is a page of its own, full screen on a phone, with a back link that returns to the list without saving; saving returns to the list with the saved row highlighted. Give it a date and a memo, then type its **Amount** once (Spent or Received; on a transfer between two accounts, what moved) and pick the accounts it touched and the categories it was for. One account takes the Amount, and so does one category line. Two accounts and no category is a transfer: the first account gets the Amount and the second the opposite. Anything more (a third account, or a category beside two accounts) shows an amount box on each line, filled in with what it held, and so do two or more category lines; change those boxes as you need, and the Amount no longer rewrites them. Remove lines with the remove button beside them and the box goes away again. Category lines can be left off entirely — the money just arrives unassigned. The Ledger shows "unassigned" only when the transaction moved budget money; a transaction that moved none, such as a payment between two on-budget accounts, shows "—". The app only refuses to save when the category lines don't add up to what the account lines moved on-budget; everything else is recorded as entered, including spending that takes an account below its on-budget floor. Add a category by name from the same screen. The Transactions list is grouped by month, newest month first, newest transaction first inside each. A month's header shows the sum of its account lines (a negative total in red, with its minus); click it to collapse or open the month, or use **Expand all** / **Collapse all**; which months are collapsed is remembered on this device, and saving a transaction opens its month if it was collapsed. Tap the **Date** or **Payee** header to reorder the rows inside every month (the arrow shows the order; transactions with no payee come last when sorting by Payee) - months themselves always stay newest first. Narrow the list with the search box (memo or payee name) and the **Account**, **Category**, **Payee**, **Domain** and **Need level** filters; every filter you set must match. Account matches a transaction with a line on that account, and while it is set each row shows only that account's lines and a month's total is that account's change. Category matches that exact category, not the ones under it. Domain and Need level match a transaction with a category line whose category has it. While filters are on, every month with a match is open (your collapsed months come back when you clear), and **Expand all** / **Collapse all** are hidden.
- Recording a transaction dated on or after an archived category, payee, or account's archive date doesn't block the save: it adds a note naming what was archived and when, and the record stands as entered. Nothing is unarchived for you.
- Pick a payee for a transaction — who it went to or came from — by searching for it in a searchable list, or leave it blank if you don't know. If the payee doesn't exist yet, add it by name right from the same box. With the keyboard, type and press Enter: it picks the exact match, otherwise the top suggestion, otherwise adds what you typed as a new payee (Enter with the box empty saves as usual); Escape closes the list. Picking a payee also fills in the category, account and split from the last payment to that payee (the category from its largest line, the account the most money left, and Paid by stays Me), but only into fields you haven't filled yet, never when the payment is marked as paying a bill, and never when you open a saved transaction. A payee you've never paid fills in nothing, and anything archived since is left blank. On a payment with no split, the filled category takes the Amount you type. Everything filled in can be changed or cleared before saving. After a save, the payee box and memo start blank again.
- Mark which bill, and which due date, a payment paid: when a category line in the Transactions form is on a category with a recurring bill, the form says "This pays Rent, due Oct 1" (the bill's earliest due date with no payment marked yet). Click "Confirm" to mark it, pick another of the bill's due dates from the list (a date that already has a payment is labelled "already paid"; a second payment on it is fine, e.g. rent in two transfers), or click "Leave unlinked". The app never marks a bill on its own: spending on the category with no mark doesn't count as paying it, and one marked payment counts as paying that due date whatever its amount. Open a payment later to change the due date or click "Remove link". If the payment has no line on the bill's category the save still goes through, with a note. A bill can't be archived on or before the date of its latest marked payment. The Transactions list shows "pays Rent, due Oct 1" under a marked payment.
- Fix a mistake: click any transaction in the list to open it in the same form, change anything, and save — every balance shown for every date reflects the correction right away. Delete it from the same form if it shouldn't exist at all (you'll be asked to confirm). If it was a paycheque or one-off income that assigned money on the pay screen, the confirmation also offers "Also remove the money moves this pay assigned," checked by default, same choice Income's own "Delete this pay" gives; leave it unchecked to keep those moves in place, unlinked. The one line you can't delete this way is an account's opening-balance adjustment; that's fixed with a balance check or backfill instead.

## Income

- Add a named pay on the Income page: give it a name ("Salary", "Gig work"), optionally a payee, how often it lands (monthly, quarterly, every 6 months, yearly, or every N weeks), the next payday you know about, an expected gross (optional), a low and high expected net, the category it lands in, the account it lands in, and any expected deductions (category + amount). If your income is lumpy, set it to monthly and estimate the least and most you receive in a month. Each row shows its next payday, computed from the date you gave rolled forward by the cadence — recording a paycheque never moves it. Click "Edit" on a row to change any of it; "Archive" removes it (its name frees up for reuse), and "Show archived" brings it back to unarchive.
- Click "Record" on a named pay to record a paycheque for it: the date defaults to its next payday and can be changed. Enter the gross and any deductions (pre-filled from the named pay, add or remove rows freely) — or turn off "This paycheque has deductions" and enter the net directly. Net shows large at the top. Every goal bound to this named pay shows up pre-filled with what it needs by the next payday: "Retain income" for a commitment that adds a percentage of net (computed live off net, with a Remove per row); "Bills" for recurring bills, soonest due first (a bill set aside whole is still listed but filled in with nothing until its last payday, and reads "set aside whole on Oct 24"); "Funding rules" for fixed-amount and refill-to-level commitments, each with a checkbox to skip it this time (a fixed amount that repeats monthly, quarterly, every 6 months or yearly is pre-filled with its share of what is still missing, spread over the paydays left before it is due, and reads like "$100.00 of $200.00 due Oct 31"; one set to each payday is pre-filled with the full amount); and "Goal set-asides" for targets. Add anything one-off this period, and an amount for any other category under "Everything else" (last period's actual shows as a grey hint). If the running "Left over" goes negative, the goal rows re-sort so the ones you'd most want to trim (wants, then nice-to-haves, then shoulds, then needs) show first, and a "Short by" section appears listing every other category that still has money in it; click "Cover from this" on one to add the shortfall (or what it has, if less) as an amount you can edit or clear — nothing moves until you click "Record", and what you've already covered from a category isn't offered again. Categories you're already funding this screen aren't offered, so you can't cover a goal from itself. "Left over" turns red if you've assigned more than this paycheque brings in. Click "Record" to save: it writes one transaction and moves the deductions, the rest to ready to assign, and whatever you typed into the goal rows and the two distribution lists. A pay that's already recorded shows a "recorded" pill instead of "upcoming", and offers "Delete this pay", which removes the transaction and, if you leave the checkbox ticked, the money moves it made too.
- Re-open a recorded pay to correct it to your stub and place the difference: on the Income page a pay already recorded for its next payday shows "Re-open" instead of "Record", and any pay's transaction on Transactions has a "Re-open pay" link. It's the same screen, showing what you recorded rather than what your goals would suggest now: every category's amount sits in its goal's block, or in "Everything else", or in "Short by" if it was a cover, and "One-off this period" starts empty (its lines are under "Everything else"). Correct the gross, the deductions, or just the net; "Left over" is the new net minus what you'd assigned, so the difference shows there for you to place. A category archived since shows "(archived)" and can't be changed. Click "Record" to save: it edits the same transaction (the date stays the payday, no second one is written) and then re-saves the money moves. If the second step fails you'll see the error and the difference sits in the income category until you click "Record" again.
- On the pay screen, "Everything else" lists every category that doesn't have a block of its own on this pay: categories with no goal, and categories whose goal is paid by another pay or by none. A category with a goal shows the goal's name and due date beside it (no date if the goal has none). None of these rows is pre-filled; the amount box starts empty, with last period's actual as a grey hint. On a one-off income every category, goal or not, is listed there, since nothing binds to a one-off.
- Click "One-off income" beside the named pays for a bonus, a tax refund, or a single gig payment you want to split across categories instead of assigning one category at a time. It opens the same screen with no named pay behind it: nothing is pre-filled, so pick the income category and destination account, and enter the gross (or net). The header shows only the date, there's no period range, and the goal blocks (Retain income, Bills, Funding rules, Goal set-asides) don't show up since nothing binds to a one-off. "Everything else" hints show last calendar month's actual instead of last period's. Everything else — the one-off row, Short by, Left over, Record — works the same as it does for a named pay. Each visit is a fresh form: there's no "recorded" pill or "Delete this pay" for a one-off, and recording two one-offs on the same date is fine. To correct or delete a one-off you've recorded, open its transaction on Transactions.

## Balance checks

- Check balance (money accounts) or update value (assets and investments): from the account list, click "Check balance" / "Update value", type the real balance (tap **− Owed** if it is a balance you owe) and a date, and optionally pick a category for the difference — otherwise it lands in ready to assign. A check on a tracking account never touches ready to assign and offers no category: the adjustment is recorded on the account only. On an account with linked categories, the difference is split across them in proportion to their balances (rounded to the cent, any leftover cent to the largest); edit the amounts before saving (tap **− Spent** / **+ Received** to flip one), or clear them to pick one category instead. If it already matches the ledger, the check is recorded with no adjustment. The opening balance isn't a check: an account reads "opened <date>" until you check it, and entries recorded before then get no "predates" note or "entries added since" count. Nothing ever locks: the account shows "balance checked <date>" (or "value updated <date>") — the latest check on or before the "Show as of" date, and none if there isn't one — and if you later save a transaction dated on or before that check, the badge picks up an "N entries added since" count.
- Typed a check wrong? Click "Undo check" next to "balance checked <date>" to remove it and any adjustment it made — the account's balance goes back to what it was before, and you check again when you're ready. An account's opening balance can't be undone this way; fix it with a backfilled transaction instead.

## Integrity check

- Open the integrity check page any time to see any transaction whose category lines don't add up, or whose on-budget amount would come out differently if it were saved again under an account's current on-budget/tracking side, or that were saved before the opening balance's date but weren't absorbed into it (older rows from before backfills could be entered in any order), or whose pool cover is out of date (you added an earlier spend in the same category after saving it, so the pool would now cover a different amount; the row names the category and the pool, and its Re-save fixes it without touching any other row). Nothing runs in the background and nothing is dev-only — it's a page like any other. Fix one row at a time with its "Re-save" button, which writes the transaction again through the normal save path; there's no "fix everything" button on purpose.

## Backups

These steps are for the Raspberry Pi running the production stack. In every command below, `dc` means `docker compose -f docker-compose.prod.yml`, run from the app folder (the one holding `docker-compose.prod.yml` and `.env`). If you like, type `alias dc='docker compose -f docker-compose.prod.yml'` first.

- A backup is one plain file holding a full copy of your data. The `backup` service in the production stack takes one every night at 03:00 UTC and saves it in the `backups` folder inside the app folder, named `nightly-<date and time>.sql` (for example `nightly-20261019T030000Z.sql`). It keeps the newest 14 and deletes older `nightly-` files. If a night's backup fails, it says so in `dc logs backup` and leaves the earlier files alone. The `pre-migrate-` files the app saves before an update are separate and are never deleted for you.
- Download the newest backup from the app: open Settings and press "Download latest backup". It shows when that backup was taken ("Last backup: ...") and saves the file to your phone or computer, so you can keep a copy off the Pi. Before the first nightly backup exists it says "No backup yet" and offers no download button. To restore one, see the next bullet (from Settings) or the command below (when the app opens on the restore screen).
- Restore a backup from the app: open Settings, find the list under "Restore a backup" (every file in the `backups` folder, newest first, each with the date it was taken: nightly, `pre-migrate-`, `pre-restore-`) and press Restore beside the one you want. A confirmation names its date and says everything you have recorded since is replaced and a backup of your current data is taken first; press "Replace my data" to go ahead, or Cancel to do nothing. While it runs the button reads "Restoring…" and the page is locked; when it finishes the page reloads on the restored data. If it cannot (another backup or restore is running, the file was cut short, or it comes from a newer version of the app) the message says why and nothing is changed. To undo a restore, restore the new `pre-restore-` file the same way. Afterwards check your balances and Data check, as in the steps below.
- Add a backup file from your phone or computer: open Settings and press "Add a backup file", then pick a `.sql` backup (for example one you copied off the Pi). It reads "Adding…" while it uploads, then the file appears in the list under "Restore a backup", named `uploaded-<date and time>.sql`. Only a finished file is accepted; one that was cut short (see the tip below for how to tell) is refused with a message and nothing is added. Adding a file only puts it in the list: nothing is restored until you press Restore beside it. `uploaded-` files are never deleted for you, are not counted among the newest 14 nightly backups, and do not count as a backup for the empty-database protection below.
- Export your data as spreadsheet files: open Settings and press "Export data". You get one zip, named `export-YYYYMMDD.zip` for the day it was made so exports kept side by side do not overwrite each other, with one file per table (accounts, categories, transactions and so on), each a CSV that opens in any spreadsheet. Everything is in it, including archived items and transactions dated in the future, whatever date "Show as of" is on. Amounts are plain dollars (`-12.34`) in a column named without `_cents`, dates are `YYYY-MM-DD`, an empty cell means nothing was entered, and each reference (a category, an account, a payee) has its name beside its id. This is a one-way copy for your own use; there is no import, and it is not a backup to restore from.
- Take a backup right now from the app: open Settings and press "Back up now". It makes the same kind of `nightly-` file as the nightly job (it counts toward the newest 14), then "Last backup" shows the new time, so "Download latest backup" includes what you just recorded. It works the same before the first nightly backup exists. If it fails, the message says so and your earlier backups are untouched.
- The nightly job, `backup-nightly.sh once` and "Back up now" skip, and say so, when the database holds no transactions while `nightly-` backups exist (for instance after a wiped Pi, before you restore). An empty backup taken then would push a real one out of the newest 14, so nothing is written and nothing is deleted. "Back up now" shows the message instead of a new "Last backup" time; the nightly job and the command line print it. Restore your backup (steps below) and backups resume. With no `nightly-` files in the `backups` folder, a first backup is taken as usual.
- Copy backups off the Pi yourself from time to time (for example to your computer or a USB stick); a backup that only lives on the Pi is lost if the Pi is.
- To tell a backup finished: run `grep "PostgreSQL database dump complete" backups/<file>`. A finished file contains the line `-- PostgreSQL database dump complete`, and its last line starts with `\unrestrict`. A file without them was cut short; don't rely on it. The nightly job checks for this line and throws away a dump that lacks it.
- Take a backup from the command line when the app is down (Back up now needs the app running):

```bash
dc exec backup /app/backup-nightly.sh once
```

- Restore a backup, on the Pi that was wiped and rebuilt or any fresh stack. This replaces everything in the app with what the file holds. You need the app folder with its `.env` file, and Docker installed.
  1. Put the backup file in the `backups` folder inside the app folder (create the folder if it is missing). Below it is called `nightly-20261019T030000Z.sql`; use your file's real name.
  2. Stop the app if it is running: `dc stop backend` (on a fresh stack it may say there is nothing to stop; that is fine). Then run the restore with the file's name only, not a path:

```bash
dc run --rm --entrypoint python backend -m app.restore nightly-20261019T030000Z.sql
```

  The command refuses, before changing anything, if the file is missing, was cut short (no `-- PostgreSQL database dump complete` line), contains a command meant for the database tool rather than data, or comes from a newer version of the app than the one you are running (update the app first, then restore). If another backup or restore is running it refuses too. Otherwise it first saves your current data as `pre-restore-<date and time>.sql` in the `backups` folder, then replaces the data with the file's, and brings an older backup up to date for the current app. If the load fails, your data is as it was. To undo a restore, restore the `pre-restore-` file the same way. `pre-restore-` files are never deleted for you.
  3. Start the whole app: `dc up -d`
  4. Check it came back. Open the app, set "Show as of" to a date you know, and compare account balances and category available amounts with what you expect (a past screenshot or the old app). Then open Settings, Data check: it should report nothing wrong. If a balance or the data check is off, don't record anything; repeat the restore with an earlier backup.
- If the app opens on the restore screen: the database is empty (for instance a wiped Pi) while nightly backups of your data sit in the `backups` folder, so the app shows only the Backups section of Settings, under "This database is empty but backups exist. Restore one to carry on.", instead of starting a blank app. Press Restore beside the backup you want (the newest nightly one is first), then "Replace my data"; the page reloads on your data. You can also add a backup file you copied off the Pi, or download the newest one, from this screen. Nothing else in the app works until you restore. The backend log (`dc logs backend`) still prints the exact command line restore with the newest backup's name filled in (steps above), if you prefer it. To start over on purpose, move the `nightly-` files out of the `backups` folder and restart the app.
