# v0.3 full review — Open Source Finance App

2026-10-24 to 2026-10-25 · Architect session with Ben

> Kept as a record of the review that produced the 2026-10-24 and 2026-10-25 decision log lines. The live findings were made on a copy of real data; every personal name, payee and amount from it has been replaced with a generic description, because this repository is public and personal financial data is never committed (`CLAUDE.md`). The numbered decisions and the issues they filed are the part that matters; DESIGN.md is still the only source of truth.

## Summary

The app does what DESIGN.md says for recording, envelopes, pay, splits, debt and backups, and the backend is the strongest part of it. The review found two problems that changed a money number, a phone UI that is a desktop table squeezed down, screens drifting from the UI rules, and two needs the design had no mechanism for.

- **Ready to assign read too high** by a split member's balance, because their receivable predated the Owed to me link and was never linked, and nothing in the app said so.
- **Pool cover went stale when backfilling newest-first.** Reproduced on the copy: a category at −$30 while its pool still held $80. The integrity check missed it; a re-save fixed it.
- **The phone was the weak surface.** Lists were desktop tables; the first row started 410–575 px down an 844 px screen; two pages scrolled sideways.
- **Screens drifted from the rules**, not the backend: no running leftover on the pay screen, no assigned or spent on categories, several controls per row, $0.00 shown while loading, refusals in cents.
- **The data showed two needs the design lacked:** which account a bill is paid from (links were being used for it, producing false notes), and recurring savings with a Record link.

## Design drift

The backend matched DESIGN.md closely; the drift was almost all on the screens.

| # | What the app did | What DESIGN.md said | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| D1 | A category row showed Available only | Goals: "the category row itself shows assigned, spent, available" | #401 |
| D2 | Rows carried several controls (Accounts: Check balance, Archive, Undo check; Income: Record, Edit, Archive; Split expenses and Payees: Edit / Archive) | Row actions: one control at most, Archive at the foot of the side panel | #398 |
| D3 | Categories painted $0.00 on every row, with no headline, until its summary arrived | Loading: placeholder rows, no word | #400 |
| D4 | An unlinked receivable still counted in Ready to assign, and nothing said so | 2026-10-19 log claimed "the reminder flags the gap"; the reminder only compares linked groups | #391 |
| D5 | Refusal messages spoke in cents and ids ("Category lines sum to 1234 cents", "Unknown category id(s)") | Wording: names and dates, never ids; dollars only at display | not filed |
| D6 | The envelope step refused an archived category | Recording on an archived entity is a note, never a refusal | not filed |
| D7 | The nav showed a Review header with nothing under it | Seven Review pages named, none built | expected until Reports |
| D8 | The pay screen showed Left over once, at the bottom of a 5,600 px page | "a running leftover down the right edge … the single most useful thing on the screen" | #399 |

**Mechanisms used for a job they weren't built for.**

- **Links used as "this bill is charged to".** Three subscription categories were linked to the credit card that pays them, and two recurring-payment categories to the chequing account they leave from. Links mean "this envelope's money physically lives here", so both accounts carried a permanent, untrue reminder, every card purchase asked which envelope the money left, and a shared bill on that card had two linked lines, which stopped the Owed to me step pre-filling. Decided: no "paid from" field; help text on *Money lives in* (#392).
- **A savings transfer modelled as a bill.** A biweekly deposit to a registered savings account is a recurring bill, so it reads "overdue" until recorded. Decided: it is a bill (an automatic transfer leaving the budget on a schedule); no change.

## Data correctness

The core arithmetic is sound: one write path, the invariant checked on every save, `budget_cents` a pure function of the line, and the integrity check was empty on the copy.

**Pool cover depended on entry order.** Reproduced with two throwaway categories:

1. A category holding $30, drawing on a pool holding $100.
2. Record a $50 spend on Oct 20 first: the save says "covered $20.00 from the pool".
3. Then backfill a $30 spend on Oct 10. It needs no cover on its own date.
4. Result: the category sits at −$30 while the pool still holds $80. Entered oldest-first, the category is $0 and the pool $50.
5. The integrity check reports nothing. Re-saving the Oct 20 row repairs it.

Cover is computed once from the balance on the transaction's own date, so any row entered or edited earlier in time afterwards leaves later cover stale. Decided: a *stale cover* integrity finding, repaired by re-save (#390). The envelope step for owed money ("only the part above zero counts") is computed the same way and was not tested.

**Smaller things**

- A pre-migrate dump was written on every container start and never pruned (102 files in the dev folder). Decided in #402.
- `data_steps.py` still runs the #42 opening-adjustment backfill on every start; harmless, needed by no install now.
- `Transactions.jsx` (1,270 lines, 41 `useState`s), `Accounts.jsx` (1,032) and `PayRecord.jsx` (903) are where the next bug will hide. Split first in #394.

## UX walk-through

The desktop app worked and read honestly; the phone was where it struggled, because every list was a desktop table with columns hidden. Measured in a 390 × 844 frame, then confirmed with screenshots on desktop and a squeezed window.

| Page | Worked well | Got in the way | First row on a phone |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Quick add | Pick a payee and category, account and date fill in, with a line saying where they came from; Save at thumb height | The amount starts empty even for a payee always paid the same amount; the amount field is ordinary size | n/a |
| Categories | The tree, group totals, "Food itself", absorb wording | No assigned or spent; the same pool pill repeats on every child; Domain and Need level columns stay on the phone; sideways scroll | 575 px down |
| Transactions | Grouped by month, filters, split and bill labels | Five columns on a phone, amounts broken across lines; unlabelled opening rows; the month total mixes spending, transfers and opening balances | 550 px |
| Accounts | Group by side or type, terms line, carrying note | Three controls per row; false link notes | 483 px |
| Due dates | What it promised: overdue leads, Record on the row | Five columns, every cell wrapped to three lines | 411 px |
| Income | Record opens the pay screen for the right payday | Three controls on the row; sideways scroll; nothing says whether this period's pay is recorded | 438 px |
| Split expenses | Balance, Settle up and what built it, on one page | What built it is oldest-first while Transactions is newest-first; no running balance | — |
| Settings | Back up now, restore with one confirmation | 102 restore rows push Export data off the bottom | — |

**Desktop pass.** The pay screen had no running leftover. Width worked against reading: on Categories a name and its amount sat about 1,150 px apart, on the pay screen about 1,300. Goal rows — progress, due status, Record — were set smaller and dimmer than their category and started left of its name. The Move money panel showed no available amount or goal progress. On Due dates only *overdue* used the warning colour.

**Phone pass.** Transactions was the worst page and is a bottom-bar tab. Categories put its controls first. The pay screen's top bar said "Income" rather than the pay's name and kept the bottom bar up on a long form. Quick add was the best page on a phone.

**The pattern.** On a phone, two-thirds of the first screen was the page's controls. Filters and sort headers were shared components, but six pages each hand-built their own table, so nothing decided once how a row looks on a phone.
